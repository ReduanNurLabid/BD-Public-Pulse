# Question candidates: November 2026

Research snapshot as of late November 2026. Sources are linked at the bottom. Check any number before publishing; some reports conflict.

## Where things stand

- **The BNP is in government** with a two-thirds majority since the 12 February 2026 election. Tarique Rahman is prime minister.
- **Opposition:** Jamaat-e-Islami is the main opposition and leads an 11-party alliance that includes the NCP (6 seats). The alliance has walked out of parliament and the NCP is protesting on the streets. Some NCP leaders are uneasy about the Jamaat tie-up.
- **The Awami League** has been banned since May 2025. Reports say the government may make the ban permanent by law. Hasina is in India, and Dhaka says extradition is the only route.
- **The July Charter fight:** "Yes" won the referendum, but the BNP formed a parliamentary amendment committee (chaired by the home minister) instead of the Constitution Reform Council. Jamaat and NCP call this a violation of the referendum verdict. The committee report is due in January.
- **Economy:** Inflation is around 8.6%, against a government target of 7.5% (the IMF expects higher). Load-shedding has been severe since July; an LPG cylinder went from ৳900 to ৳1,500. The Family Card scheme is the BNP's flagship, and its rollout has caused disputes. Bangladesh has asked to delay leaving LDC status to 2029. US tariffs on garments are about 25%.
- **Labour:** Garment wage protests met police and alleged ruling-party youth-wing attacks. A wage board was formed on 1 November.
- **Law and order:** Mob violence and extortion continue. ASK counts 154 mob killings from January to August. Many incidents are clashes within the BNP. Police reform recommendations have not been taken up.
- **Press:** The Cyber Security/Protection Act 2026 kept broad provisions. Journalists have been charged over corruption reporting. Bangladesh ranks 152nd on the press freedom index.
- **India:** The Ganges water treaty **expires on 12 December 2026** and renewal isn't agreed. Teesta is still unsigned.
- **Campus:** Chhatra Dal and Shibir are fighting over control of halls. Some campuses want student politics banned.
- **Local elections:** These are phased from November 2026 to mid-2027. The opposition says they keep being delayed.

**What this means for the questionnaire:** questions about July accountability are now settled history. The valuable, trending data is about **how people judge the BNP government and the opposition**, and **the live policy fights of 2026–27**.

---

## Two kinds of questions

1. **Position questions** (the scored 24): long-lasting value trade-offs, framed with today's issues. These drive the compass, the topic bars and party matching.
2. **Pulse questions** (new, recommended): 3–4 unscored questions on government and opposition performance, asked after the quiz and **repeated every month**. These make the trend line ("government approval fell 8 points since September") that journalists will quote. This needs a small new feature; see the end.

---

## Recommended position set (24)

Every line is a plain statement; there are no "ধরুন" scenarios. "Agree →" shows which side of the topic agreeing points to. **R** = worded so that agreeing points the other way (to balance acquiescence). "Likely agree / disagree" is my estimate of each camp's position, to calibrate the party weights.

## Pulse questions (monthly, unscored)

Ask these right after the result, with the same 5-point scale. Keep the wording fixed from month to month so the trend is valid.

| Code | Statement |
|---|---|
| P1 | সব মিলিয়ে বর্তমান সরকার দেশকে সঠিক পথে নিয়ে যাচ্ছে। |
| P2 | গত কয়েক মাসে আমার এলাকায় চাঁদাবাজি ও মাস্তানি কমেছে। |
| P3 | নিত্যপণ্যের দাম নিয়ন্ত্রণে সরকার সফল হচ্ছে। |
| P4 | বিরোধী জোট (জামায়াত-এনসিপি) দায়িত্বশীল বিরোধী দলের ভূমিকা পালন করছে। |

Optional rotating fifth slot for the news of the month (for example in December: "গঙ্গা চুক্তি নিয়ে সরকার দেশের স্বার্থ রক্ষা করতে পারবে।").

**Implementation cost:** one new aggregate field per pulse question per month (for example `pulse.2026-11.P1.options.4`), a small result-screen card, and a dashboard line chart. About half a day of work.

## Party model changes to consider

- Keep 5 profiles, but describe the **BNP as the governing party** and **Jamaat as the opposition leader**. The current profile text is written from the pre-election period.
- The pulse questions let you show "BNP supporters vs opposition supporters" splits on government approval, using the optional party-support question.
- The AL profile: AL is banned and can't run, but it still has supporters. Keep it, and avoid wording that assumes it's competing in elections.
